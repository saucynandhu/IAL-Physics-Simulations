Circular Motion Theory

Circular motion occurs when an object moves in a circular path at constant speed. Despite the constant speed, the object is accelerating because its direction is continuously changing.

F = mv²/r

Where F is the centripetal force, m is mass, v is velocity, and r is radius.

a = v²/r = ω²r

Where a is centripetal acceleration, ω is angular velocity (ω = v/r).

T = 2π/ω = 2πr/v

Where T is the period of one complete revolution.
