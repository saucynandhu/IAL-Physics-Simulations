Theory of Diffraction

What is Diffraction?

Diffraction is the bending of waves around obstacles or through openings. In light waves, this phenomenon creates characteristic patterns that depend on the wavelength of light and the size/shape of the aperture.

Single Slit Diffraction

When light passes through a single slit, it creates a pattern of bright and dark fringes. The central maximum is the brightest, with decreasing intensity in the side fringes.

d sin(θ) = mλ

Where:

  • d is the slit width
  • θ is the angle of diffraction
  • m is the order of the fringe
  • λ is the wavelength of light

Double Slit Interference

When light passes through two closely spaced slits, it creates an interference pattern. The bright fringes occur where the waves from both slits are in phase.

d sin(θ) = mλ

Where:

  • d is the distance between slits
  • θ is the angle of diffraction
  • m is the order of the fringe
  • λ is the wavelength of light
