Hooke's Law Theory

Basic Principle

Hooke's Law describes the relationship between the force applied to a spring and its extension:

F = -kx

Where:

  • F is the force applied (in Newtons, N)
  • k is the spring constant (in N/m)
  • x is the extension from equilibrium (in meters, m)
  • The negative sign indicates the force is restorative

Key Concepts

  • Elastic Limit: The maximum extension before permanent deformation occurs
  • Spring Constant (k):
    • Measures the stiffness of the spring
    • Higher k = stiffer spring
    • Lower k = more flexible spring
  • Simple Harmonic Motion: The oscillatory motion of a spring-mass system
  • Period of Oscillation: T = 2π√(m/k)
    • m is the mass (in kg)
    • k is the spring constant (in N/m)

Energy in Spring Systems

Spring systems involve two types of energy:

  • Potential Energy: PE = ½kx²
    • Stored in the spring when stretched or compressed
    • Maximum at maximum extension
    • Zero at equilibrium position
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv²
    • Energy of motion
    • Maximum at equilibrium position
    • Zero at maximum extension

The total energy (PE + KE) remains constant in an ideal system (no damping).

Real-World Applications

  • Car suspension systems
  • Mechanical watches
  • Shock absorbers
  • Musical instruments (piano strings)
  • Exercise equipment
  • Industrial machinery
